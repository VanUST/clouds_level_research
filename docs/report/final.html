Stereo cloud-base height from a 45 m fisheye baseline

A validated instrument, a measured anomaly, and twelve pre-registered refutations. The instrument works; the cause of the real-world failure is unattributed, and this report says so rather than inferring one.

The one-paragraph version. We built a passive stereo cloud-base-height estimator from two zenith-pointing fisheye cameras on a 45 m baseline and tested it against a co-located laser ceilometer. We first proved the instrument works by construction: a synthetic renderer, whose geometry is derived from the shipped calibration and gated against the analytic parallax, recovers a cloud layer of known height with ρ = +1.000 and 0.07 px median bias. Through that same renderer we found the shipped lowest selection rule errs by 325–1471 m on ground truth, and replaced it with lowest_mode, which errs by ≤64 m. On real data the estimator does not recover cloud base height: higher clouds produce lower reported heights, significantly and robustly. We tested and refuted twelve explanations for that inversion with pre-registered criteria. None accounts for it.

1The validated instrument

Two 180° fisheye cameras, 1920×1920, baseline 45 m, 10 s cadence, on 2025-05-23. The reference is a DOL-2 laser ceilometer whose log is stamped MSK; the image filenames are UTC; the offset is exactly +3 h, established twice independently — by the diurnal brightness envelope and by a solar ephemeris matching the Sun's image azimuth to better than 0.5° across fourteen hours.

Three calibration terms were measured rather than assumed, each by more than one method:

termmeasured valuehow
focal constant654 ± 11 px/rad (assumed: 611) five independent estimates — four-stream Sun-radius fits on both cameras and both days, plus the clear-day Sun metric

cubic lens distortiona = −0.30 in θ = (r/f)(1+a(r/f)²) fitted from the sky's own flatness requirement; stable against f; confirmed end-to-end

The synthetic control, and why it took two attempts

A renderer casts rays through the measured fisheye geometry onto a cloud plane at a known height, samples a procedural texture in metres, and warps camera 1 by the shipped affine exactly as production does. Its rig geometry was derived from the calibration, and one term took a withdrawn attempt to find: the affine maps camera 1's principal point onto camera 2's, so c2 = R·c1 + t = (1014.10, 965.03), not the image centre. With both principal points assumed central, the rendered parallax comes out negative and grows with height.

Renderer gate (named in w7_renderer.validate()): du(zenith) must equal f·atan(B/H) and fall as 1/H. Measured 29.428 / 14.715 / 7.357 px at 1000 / 2000 / 4000 m against analytic 29.410 / 14.713 / 7.357 px. Passes exactly.

The shipped selection rule was a catastrophic defect

The production chain clustered the 2-D disparity vectors with HDBSCAN (min_cluster_size=3) and reported the lowest cluster of size ≥ 8 — an extreme low-tail order statistic of the measurement noise, and one whose value depends on how many points there are.

selectorρ(true, reported)perror range
field median+1.0000.000−56 to +24 m
lowest (shipped) +0.7000.188 (n.s.) −1471 to −325 m
lowest_mode (replacement) +1.0000.000−64 to +25 m

It reports 675 m for a 1000 m cloud, 722 m for 1500 m and 529 m for 2000 m. The replacement works on the per-match height distribution and reports the lowest prominent mode — one carrying ≥10 % of the weight and ≥50 matches — so it depends on where the data concentrate rather than how many points exist. It is now the project default.

SCOPE The control establishes capability on synthetic procedural texture at a known plane. It models geometry and occlusion. It applies no phase function, and the surface is smooth. Nothing here establishes performance on real cloud radiance.

On the real session the estimator does not recover cloud base height. Stratified by the ceilometer's own reported regime, it places higher clouds at lower heights:

4The boundary — what may and may not be claimed

What is established

The instrument recovers a geometrically known height on synthetic procedural texture through the measured fisheye geometry: ρ = +1.000, median |bias| = 0.072 px, robust to a 4× apparent-scale range and to 20 % relief. The shipped lowest rule was a defect erring by up to 1471 m on ground truth; lowest_mode errs by ≤64 m. The real-data inversion is robust and specific in its distributional signature.

What is claimed as product capability

A level consistent with the ceilometer's lower cloud population, in the low regime only:

regimecameraceilometer H1difference
low (H1 < 2750 m)2361 m2250 m+111 m
high (H1 > 3500 m)2064 m3790 m−1726 m
LIMITATION level agreement. The +111 m figure is a level statistic from 215 frames of one session, not a validated calibration. To elevate it to a demonstrated result requires (a) an independently calibrated level reference — the baseline is assumed at 45 m and never measured, and the focal constant carries ±11 px/rad, about ±1.7 % in level; (b) a second session, to exclude a coincidence at this site; and (c) demonstration that the agreement is not produced by the same unattributed process that produces the inversion at other heights.

No dynamic tracking is claimed. Within the low regime the estimator's variation is anti-correlated with the reference.

LIMITATION dynamics. ρ = −0.473 (p = 2.3e−13) per frame; −0.525 (p = 0.030) on a grid at the slower series' decorrelation time. The effective sample size is 7 (nominal 215, lag-1 ρ ≈ 0.94), so the study is underpowered and no finer structure should be claimed. A dynamics claim in either direction requires a session yielding ≥30 independent samples of both series, and an independent motion reference — no wind data exists for this session.
LIMITATION absolute cloud-base height. No absolute claim is supported for high, thin or multi-layer fields. The reference is itself bi-modal (40 % at 2250–2750 m, 53 % at 3750–4250 m) and reports up to 7010 m against a stated 10–3000 m range. Supporting such a claim requires a reference with a footprint comparable to the camera's 1956 m at 1837 m — a radar or scanning lidar — rather than the ~10 m column used here, a ~200:1 mismatch spanning 8.5 texture decorrelation lengths.
LIMITATION instrument identity. That the stereo measures "a valid but different physical quantity" than the ceilometer is a motivating hypothesis, not a result. No measurement here identifies which quantity. Establishing it requires a third instrument with an overlapping footprint and a known retrieval.
LIMITATION external validity. One usable session exists. 2025-05-22 is cloudless and yields single-digit correspondences per hour, so there is no session-level hold-out, no unseen-session validation and no drift measurement between sessions. Every result here is a time block of one day.
LIMITATION the anomaly's cause. The cause of the inversion is unattributed. Twelve mechanisms were refuted with pre-registered criteria; none accounts for the observed combination of a +1.5 px core shift with ×2.2 tail growth at a fixed IQR. It is not a claim that the cause is atmospheric, volumetric or photometric — two of those were tested and refuted, and the photometric coupling that does exist runs the wrong way.

5What would settle it

The analysis side of this question is exhausted: twelve mechanisms tested, twelve refuted, on a validated instrument. What remains is not another hypothesis but a controlled input.

  1. Bisect the renderer against the real signature. The validated renderer is now the project's most valuable asset. Any proposed mechanism must reproduce the specific signature — core +1.5 px, span ×2.2, IQR ×1.11, dv ×2.2 — and not merely a bias or a spread. Two mechanisms have already failed by producing the wrong part of the distribution.
  2. A physical target, or an instrumented session. The foreground control failed for a quantified geometric reason: an 87–247 px disparity at a 45 m baseline against a close, oblique building, unmatchable by either SIFT or RoMa. The limit is the rig, not the algorithm.
  3. A footprint-matched reference. A radar or scanning lidar co-located with the cameras would remove the 200:1 sampling mismatch that makes a level comparison between a point column and a 2 km area difficult to interpret at all.

6Methodology, and the harness post-mortem

What a claim required

No value produced by maximising over alternatives without a null that maximises identically. Effective sample size reported rather than nominal. A p-value, or an explicit statement that none exists, and the direction of any effect. Synthetic ground truth validated by an independent exact estimator before any method was judged by it.

The three harness bugs, reported as findings about the process

Each changed a scientific conclusion, and none was caught by inspection. They are given here as first-class results, because in our experience this is where the time actually goes.

what happenedwhat it changed
1 A metric that scored below its own null. The original validation searched a time shift per window and reported the maximum; a null performing the same search scored higher (0.346 vs 0.189). the project's original validation metric was withdrawn
2 A ground truth verified in the wrong frame. The first synthetic renderer was gated in the native frame; the matcher consumes the warped frame, which was never validated. It also modelled both cameras with the same orientation — a rig that does not exist. a positive control was withdrawn
3 A default argument that cost 551 m. Two scripts rendered the "same" synthetic cloud 1.5° apart, because one call passed roll_deg implicitly and the other did not. The control refused to reproduce — 1984 m against 1433 m on inputs believed identical. three hypotheses were proposed before the call sites were compared
What they have in common. Each is a case of a quantity being verified in a proxy that was not the thing consumed. The null is a proxy for the search; the native frame is a proxy for the warped frame; one conversion path is a proxy for the other. In every case the verification passed and the conclusion was still wrong. We would recommend that any pipeline claiming a synthetic control state explicitly, for each gate, which artefact the consumer actually reads.

A note on the shape of this result

Twelve refutations with pre-registered triggers, on an instrument whose capability was demonstrated independently, is a bounded null result: it does not say the measurement is impossible, it says this instrument, on this data, under every mechanism we could formulate and test, does not measure what the reference measures. The instrument is sound. The anomaly is real and specific. Its cause is open — and it is now stated as open, rather than filled with the most attractive available mechanism.
