1What we can describe

These are established. Each was measured, and most were measured more than one way because a single method had already proved unreliable once.

The optical geometry

The rig is two 180° fisheye cameras on a 45 m baseline, 1920×1920, ten-second cadence. Three things about it are now known rather than assumed:

  • The focal constant is ~654 px/rad, not the 611 the pipeline assumes (ratio 1.070 ± 0.018). The assumed value is ξ0/π, which presumes the 180° field maps exactly onto the inscribed circle; the circle actually measures 1078–1156 px, not 960. Every height is therefore currently ~7 % low, purely multiplicatively.
  • A residual roll of about −1.6° between the rectified images. Removing it collapses the within-frame disparity spread 4–5×.
  • A cubic lens distortion, a = −0.30, in θ = (r/f)(1 + a(r/f)²). The pipeline ignores it entirely. It is large: about 16 % angular compression at the crop corner.

The reference's structure

The ceilometer's lowest reported layer is bi-modal: 40 % of samples between 2250 and 2750 m, 53 % between 3750 and 4250 m, and almost nothing between. Only 3.8 % of consecutive steps change by more than 500 m, so this is two clouds reported in long stretches, not noise. For about half the window the two instruments are not measuring the same object, which matters for any comparison regardless of how good the algorithm is.

The matched population

Correspondences are cloud-driven: the clear day yields single-digit matches per hour against 1,100–3,700 on the cloudy day. And the selectable rules differ enormously in level — lowest 1156 m, largest 1744 m, median-of-candidates 1878 m — which says the height field is broad and the reported number is a choice about which part of it to quote.

2What we cannot describe

The core result, and it is now diagnosed. There is no demonstrated agreement with the reference in level or dynamics, and the reason is no longer a mystery: the estimated height is dominated by a static, image-position dependent pattern rather than by cloud. The test is below, and it is the single most important measurement in this document.

The decisive test: does the height follow the cloud, or the pixel?

If the retrieval assigns a height to cloud, the height map must move as the cloud moves. If it assigns a height to a place in the image, the map is the same all session. The measured cloud motion moves air through the field many times over in five hours, so a static map cannot mean the cloud stood still.

Correlating the height map of the session's first half with its second half — disjoint frames, 2.5 hours apart, through which entirely different air has passed:

One mechanism explains every failure found earlier. A static map has a constant average, so the delivered series is nearly flat — the 130 m it moves against the reference's 1450 m. Every selection rule samples the same static map, so none of them helps. The residual cloud signal rides on top of a pattern that does not move, so its sign carries no information. And adding matches widens the pattern rather than adding cloud signal.

A static height pattern is a calibration signature, and it has now been characterised directly. The static part of the disparity field is the error field, because the cloud moves and the error does not. Fitting it to candidate geometric forms, as a fraction of the field's own 0.84 px standard deviation:

The dominant error is a horizontal SCALE, not the lens distortion. The linear-in-u coefficient is +0.00394 px per px, a +16.6 % change in disparity across the 640 px crop. At 1.5 km, where the entire disparity is 18.3 px, that moves the height by more than 15 % from one side of the image to the other. The distortion is real and separate: it is radial, and applying it removed about half the static amplitude.

Subtracting the exact geometry of a horizontal plane leaves a residual larger than the field it was subtracted from, so the disagreement is systematic rather than marginal. Three explanations remain live and are distinguishable: a wrong principal point, differing focal lengths between the cameras, or a confound in which clouds occupy which image regions. Fitting a correction before separating them would encode the confound, so that separation is the next measurement and the gating one for the project.

Finally, the temporal filter is unwired while the retrieval is diagnosed — it was removing 48 % of the raw variation of a signal already ~4× under-responsive, and confounding the two makes both unreadable. It is a config switch (temporal.filter_enabled), not a deletion.

Two facts sit behind that:

  • The estimate barely moves. Hourly medians range over ~130 m while the reference moves through ~1450 m.
  • Where it does respond, the sign is inverted. Stratified by the reference's own regime, the estimate sits at 1944 m when the cloud is low and 1839 m when it is high — a shift of −105 m (KS p = 3×10−24), strengthening to −154 m restricted to the inner field. The match-radius distribution is identical across regimes (227.9 vs 227.7 px), so this is not a radial-geometry artefact.

Corrected 2026-10-13, in the favourable direction. An earlier version of this report named a "1000–1400 m within-frame scatter" as the dominant error. That figure came from the uncorrected configuration. Isolated on identical matches, the roll correction halves the height spread (689 → 341 m) and the distortion alone slightly worsens it (792 m) — the same compensation seen above — while together they reduce it 2.9× to ~235 m and raise the median 31 %, from 1441 m to 1886 m against a lower reference layer near 2400 m. The gap to the reference therefore halves, to ~514 m, before the ~7 % focal correction. The flatness remains in the height field rather than the selection rule, but the field is 2.9× narrower than it appeared.

3How it was verified — and what was withdrawn

The verification method matters as much as the results, because the first version of this project's evaluation was wrong in a way that produced plausible numbers.

Every claim against a null

The windowed correlation metric — the project's original validation — searched a time shift per 30-minute window and reported the largest correlation found. A null that rolls the estimate against a fixed reference and performs the same search scores higher than the observation (0.346 against 0.189 raw). The cause is statistical: a window holds ~152 paired samples, but the reference's autocorrelation leaves ~3.4 independent ones, and maximising over 121 shifts of three points is selection, not measurement. Every windowed correlation figure is withdrawn.

Synthetic ground truth gets an independent recovery check

The sub-pixel and differential matcher tests build ground truth by translating a real image by a known amount. Recovery of those shifts was confirmed with phase correlation — an exact estimator with no learned prior — at ratio 0.88–1.36, so the failures the matchers show are theirs and not the test's.

A proxy metric was caught rewarding the defect it was meant to detect

The first matcher evaluation ranked candidates by a plane-fit residual. It put MASt3R first. A direct test of differential sensitivity at the working disparity — can the matcher resolve a 0.5 px change on a 20 px disparity — gives it a slope of 0.000: no response at all. A consistently quantised field satisfies a plane fit as well as an accurate one. That is why the test, not the proxy, now decides.

Reproducibility is enforced, not assumed

The default matcher path reproduces the frozen reference correspondences 38 of 38 frames byte-for-byte, re-verified after every source edit. The optimisation that made inference 2.03× faster was required to produce cmp-identical series.json, clusters.jsonl and metrics.json.

5What would change the picture

In order, and each is falsifiable:

  1. Establish what the matched population is. The estimate is a low-tail order statistic of a broad, noisy field whose level is set by the field's spread and the match count. That, not the correlation, is the dominant unexplained error.
  2. Apply the geometry corrections together. Exact triangulation without the distortion is worse than the paraxial formula (19.1 % against 4.1 %) because the two errors partially cancel. They must ship as a pair.
  3. Re-test the multi-layer question under the corrected geometry, and decompose the level offset only after that.
  4. Build the spatial-sampling model and decide whether a level comparison between a 1956 m footprint and a 10 m column is defensible at all.
The one thing the project does not yet know: what the matched population actually is. Until that is answered, neither the level offset nor the single-versus-bi-modal-layer question can be interpreted.
