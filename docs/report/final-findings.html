New findings: the static field, the shape mechanism, and the detection floor

What was learned after final.html was written — a diagnosis of the static pattern, a cross-validated search for an operation that removes it, the mechanism behind the estimator-dependence of the “inversion”, and the statistical boundary of the instrument's dynamic capability. Four previous claims are amended, three of them ours.
Summary. The static spatial pattern is dominated by a linear gradient in u (66 % of its variance) rather than by a radial law (25 %), though the two are correlated and it is the radial part that survives in disparity but not in height. A cross-validated search over six correction families and three estimators removes part of the pattern and recovers none of the dynamics. The estimator-dependence of the inversion is explained by a collapse in the distribution's skewness (2.27 → 0.73) while its dispersion is unchanged: the median and the mode move in opposite directions (+13 % against −12 %), which is what order statistics do to a shape-shifting field. And the disparity field does carry a correctly-signed dynamic correlation of ≈ −0.37 which lies below the detection floor of this session — the obstacle is sample size, not absence, and it is surmountable in ≈ 8 hours of data.

1What the static field depends on

Regressing the per-cell static disparity on candidate predictors answers the question directly, and it corrects the framing: the pattern is not primarily radial.

A linear gradient in u accounts for 66 % of the variance; radius accounts for 25 %. They are correlated because u ≤ r, which is why the radial profile looks convincingly monotone — 14.51 px at the centre rising to 16.57 px at r ≈ 420, a 14 % rise — while actually being a consequence of the u-gradient as much as a radial law of its own.

And the radial structure largely cancels in height. Applying the measured corrections (f = 654, a = −0.30, exact triangulation), corr(radius, height) falls to −0.146, with median heights of 1975 / 1955 / 1930 / 1918 / 1947 m across the radius — a 3 % spread against the disparity's 14 %. Disparity and height are not the same quantity, and the radial dependence lives mostly in the former.

Finding 1 — the sign depends on the estimator

The mode estimator is correctly signed out of sample (+0.053 uncorrected, +0.217 with the best correction). The median and the 25th percentile are inverted (−0.347 to −0.246). The best correction moves the level by +0.065 and leaves the sign on the correct side.

Corrected after review: these figures come from blocked + embargo cross-validation. The first version drew folds at random, which on a series with lag-1 autocorrelation 0.934 placed each held-out frame's near-duplicates in the training set; that leaked +0.070 into the headline (+0.287 against the honest +0.217). See experiments/w10/CODE_REVIEW.md.

Finding 2 — no correction recovers the dynamics

Across all eighteen combinations the first-difference correlation lies within ±0.19 with no consistent sign, and the best level correction does not improve it (uncorrected +0.004 against +0.016 corrected). The most expressive family tested — a free offset per 20 × 20 px cell plus a plane, fitted on 80 % of frames — would recover a real signal hiding under a fixed additive pattern. It does not.

The static field is diagnosable and partly removable; removing it does not recover the dynamics. The anomaly cannot be described as “a static field masking a real signal” without qualification — an expressive correction does not reveal the signal.

3The shape mechanism — why the median inverts

The estimator-dependence is not a defect of any statistic. It is a change in the distribution's shape between regimes, and it is measured.

The median and the mode move in opposite directions while the standard deviation is unchanged. The distribution does not widen or narrow between regimes; it changes shape, its skewness collapsing by 68 %. A statistic tracking the bulk (the mode) and one tracking cumulative mass (the median) must diverge when skewness changes — this is what order statistics do, not a failure of either.

It also unifies the synthetic and real results. In the synthetic wind tunnel the cloud's disparity distribution has a fixed shape across heights, so no statistic is biased by a shape change and only the tail-sensitive rule fails — which is exactly what was observed (lowest erred by up to 1471 m while lowest_mode erred by ≤64 m). In the real atmosphere the shape does change between regimes, so the median and the mode diverge. The same mechanism explains both, and the direction of the failure is set by the sign of the skewness change.

At 300 s binning — the reference's own decorrelation time — the disparity field carries a weak, correctly-signed dynamic correlation.

The observed −0.37 lies below the 0.470 detection floor of the design. Establishing a correlation of 0.37 at 80 % power needs neff ≈ 60, i.e. ≈ 8 hours of binned data.

The honest statement is not “the instrument has no dynamic capability” but “the dynamic signal, if present, is at or below what this session can detect.” That converts a closed question into a concrete and cheap experiment.

5Amendments to the record

Four earlier claims are amended. Three are ours.

AMENDMENT — the dynamics were not recovered, so the field carries no dynamic information

was W9 measured per-frame first differences on held-out folds and found |ρ| < 0.17, which was read as the disparity field holding no recoverable temporal signal.

now at 300 s binning the same data gives a consistent correctly-signed correlation of about −0.37 (nine of eleven statistics correctly signed, central five averaging −0.369). The earlier reading was too strong: binning suppresses the per-frame matcher noise that dominated the per-frame analysis. The boundary is a detection floor, not an absence.

AMENDMENT — the search metric

was the first version of the cross-validated search correlated the disparity with the reference and reported +0.409 as its best result.

now a correct estimator needs the disparity to fall as the cloud rises, so that was the inversion scored as a success. The statistic is now height-ordered and every reported ρ carries the conventional sign. Caught by the control's sign rather than by inspection.

AMENDMENT — the duration of a decisive session

was “>15 hours” was proposed in review, and this project's own W10 stated neff ≈ 55.

now the Fisher-z relation gives neff = 60.3 for a correlation of 0.37 at 80 % power, and the binned series yields one independent sample per 449 s, so a decisive session needs ≈ 7.5 h. The correction is a factor of two and it makes the recommended experiment cheaper than it appeared.

AMENDMENT — the cause of the static field

was the pattern was described in earlier drafts as radial, and partly attributed to an unattributed interaction between the atmosphere and the matcher's priors.

now it is dominated by a linear gradient in u (66 % against radius's 25 %), and it is not primarily radial. The three specific mechanisms of the atmospheric family that were proposed were each tested and refuted. The cause of the residual remains unattributed, and that is the correct description.
