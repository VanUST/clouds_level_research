The Kalman filter does what it is claimed to do — it smooths a jumpy series and

reproduce.

## Why the correlation claims fail

**The published correlations are reproducible only by consulting the ground
truth.** The shipped `parse_algorithm_logs` selected, per frame, the cluster
whose height was closest to the ceilometer reading. Replicating that on this
run's own cluster dump:

| Selection rule | mean r | windows r>0.5 | max r | median height |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **ground-truth guided (quarantined)** | **0.759** | **0.80** | **0.990** | 2,938 m |
| lowest cluster | 0.202 | 0.05 | 0.647 | 1,156 m |
| lowest reliable (size ≥ 8) | 0.197 | 0.15 | 0.757 | 1,156 m |
| largest cluster | 0.161 | 0.05 | 0.579 | 1,744 m |
| lowest, size ≥ 20 / 50 / 100 / 200 | 0.183–0.259 | ≤ 0.20 | — | ≈ 1,230 m |

No ceilometer-free rule exceeds mean r ≈ 0.26. The published band (0.55–0.67) is
reached only by the leaky rule, which also overshoots it. No selection rule in
the validated path may consult the ceilometer, and none does.

**The written work and the figure disagree with each other.** The text says the
`lowest` strategy was used; the figure's legend labels its raw series "Raw
dominant cluster" (= largest), which scores *worse* here (0.161 vs 0.202). The
text states a 640×640 crop; the shipped configuration used 320×320. Both were
tested: 640 is the configuration that reproduces the non-correlation claims
(spatial rejection 15.45 %, filtered autocorrelation 0.978); at 320 they are
10.2 % and 0.968.

**The figure's own metadata is more informative than any script in the tree.**
Its title reads "18 x 30min windows stitched", its inset reads
"Mean r = 0.608 | r>0.5: 13/18 (72 %)" and it plots a Reliability Index. Nothing
in the current source produces that figure; the driver that did was deleted in
`96c5f75` and is deliberately not restored
Its window count (18) is consistent with 30-minute windows and a 15-minute hop
over 5 hours, which is what this record uses — and it still does not reproduce.

## Open and unverified

- **Correlation cannot be used to set the instrument clock.** The wide shift scan
  on this run puts its maximum at −3.25 h (r = 0.321) relative to the
  already-shifted series, i.e. near the *image* clock, while `r = −0.155` at zero.
  With peak correlations this low the scan is not decisive, so it neither
  confirms nor refutes the +3 h. The +3 h rests on the physical evidence — the
  diurnal brightness envelope, recorded in
  [`data/instruments.md`](../data/instruments.md) — not on this scan.
- **The optical-versus-geometric offset was not quantified.** The paper's
  explanation for the vertical offset was not tested here; only agreement in
  dynamics was.
- **No calibration exists for 2025-05-23**, so an unknown part of the residual is
  calibration drift (documented as up to 30 px ≈ 900 m).
- **`run_iterative_refinement` and the MatchAnything backend were not exercised.**

## What would settle it

The missing driver's parameters — window length, hop, selection strategy and
Kalman constants — are recoverable from commit `96c5f75`'s parent if the exact
published number must be traced. Until then the honest statement is: on this
data, with this configuration, the published ceilometer correlations are **not
reproduced**, and a mechanism that would inflate them is present in the shipped
analysis code.
